Look up

The carbs and glycemic load of any portion from its label and a kitchen scale, and the glycemic index of more than 180 foods.

The label calculator

Carbs and load of a portion

Read the first three from the Nutrition Facts, weigh the food, and add a glycemic index from the list for the load.

GI55 or less · low56–69 · medium70 or more · high
GL10 or less · low10–20 · medium20 or more · high

Glycemic load = GI × net carbs ÷ 100, where net carbs are the total less fiber. The GL bands are set for one serving.

Settings

Six choices, saved in this browser. Change any of them and every number follows.

01

Carbs to count

The number the plate and the totals lead with. Most carb counting uses the label’s Total Carbohydrate; net carbs take the fiber off, which is also what glycemic load is built on.

02

Carb choices

Shows the count in exchange units as well as grams. In the US one carb choice is 15 g.

03

Carb budget for a meal

If your care team has given you a carbohydrate target for each meal, enter it and the plate is measured against it. Leave it empty for none.

g per meal

04

Count by

Whether the plate takes servings or a weight from a kitchen scale. Either way each food names what one serving weighs and how to weigh it — oats dry, fruit peeled.

05

Your body weight

Sets the protein target at 0.8 g per kg. Leave it empty for the default of 64 g, an 80 kg (177 lb) adult.

06

Added sugar limit

The American Heart Association’s daily maximum: 36 g for men, 25 g for women.

Privacy

Kept in this browser

Your settings and plate are saved under meal.settings and meal.plate in this browser’s own storage. Nothing is sent anywhere, and clearing the browser’s site data clears them.

Install

Keep it on your phone

Kept on a home screen or in a dock, it opens in its own window and works with no connection.
